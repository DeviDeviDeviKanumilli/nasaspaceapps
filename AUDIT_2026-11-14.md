### P0-4 Zone references double-counted cells and used a circular yardstick
- **Issue:** v1 concatenated overlapping 10 km lunar windows (2.5 km step), counting interior cells up
  to 16×; the yardstick compared each lunar window with a reference that contained it.
- **Consequence:** biased references (flat-zone 400 m median was 7.8°, correct value 8.7°) and an
  optimistic yardstick.
- **Files:** `build_zone_refs.py` (v2), `zone_report.json`; regression test "zone pooling uses unique cells".
- **Correction (implemented):** unique-cell pooling per site; leave-one-site-out yardstick; threshold
  sensitivity. New values: flat context — 69 windows, LOSO yardstick median 1.56°, p90 2.35°; steep
  context — 257 windows, median 3.21°, p90 7.62°. US: 5 windows closer than the flat median, 90 inside
  its p90; steep: 0 / 92. Threshold sensitivity: changing 60%→50/70% (flat) or 40%→30/50% (steep) moves
  the reference by 0.8–1.4°, i.e. about as much as the typical within-zone spread — **thresholds matter**.
- **Labels (implemented in metadata/app):** "team-defined terrain-context classes, not NASA landing or
  habitat classifications". The 10° (HLS) and 20° (EVA walking) numbers apply at metre-to-tens-of-metres

### P0-5 Docs claimed one common grid; the pathways use different grids
- **Issue:** terrain = 10 km squares (local AEQD, 5 km step); rock type = 0.1° × 0.1°/cos(lat) boxes
  (~11.1 km, half-box step; integer rounding of the longitude width makes them up to ~10% wider at high

- **Consequence:** a candidate could appear to have "both" measurements over different ground.

  Docs: "one global grid" → "two independent grids, combined only by footprint overlay".
- **Evidence:** `cross_us.json`; e.g. only 4 of 50 Jezero-type geology candidates have terrain measured
  (US run only; others "not measured").

### P1-5 Masking strictness (by design, document it)
- A cell's 3.2 km disc must be completely unmasked to get a slope, so windows survive only where the
  ground is bare over several km. This makes the terrain pathway conservative and arid-biased. The rock-
  type pathway and the regional lidar run are the counterweights. Say so on the coverage screen.

### P1-6 Measurement consistency notes (no code change)
- Moon: 20 m LOLA averaged to 100 m (effective resolution ~16 m median → fine). Earth: GLO-90 averaged to
  100 m in a local AEQD grid (baseline 400 m ≥ 4× effective resolution). Mars: 200 m blend resampled to
  ~213 m square cells; only 1.6/3.2 km baselines; effective resolution unknown → Mars terrain is weak
  evidence (InSight best match 0.02° = "also flat").
- Roughness = RMS residual divided by n (not n−3): biased low by 1–3% at small discs (test: 0.48 for
  σ = 0.5 m). Same on all bodies at the same grid; negligible in log10 units.
- W1 from 50 quantiles: max error 0.015° vs exact (test).

### P1-7 Field investigation that adds evidence (prepared)
- `field_prep.py` → `figs/nottingham_map.png`, `nottingham_stations.csv/json`: a **pre-registered
  transect** (10 stations, 100 m apart, from 400 m inside the mapped serpentinite unit across the mapped
  contact to the schist side) with **USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar** slope (3 m and 6 m discs) and roughness at
  each planned station, plus an on-site rule for 4 exposure stations along the barrens trails.
  The test it enables: does the mapped contact coincide with a visible change in rock/soil/vegetation and
  in metre-scale surface shape? A null result is a legitimate finding about 1:250k mapping.
- **Protocol (≈2 h):**
  1. Calibration at the car park: D435 at 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 m above a flat surface (paved lot) and a
     known-size object (1 m scale bar); record 10 s each. Plane-fit residual RMS = depth noise at that
     range; scale bar checks scale. Record camera tilt with a phone inclinometer (plain D435 has no IMU).
  2. At each station (T1–T10 where accessible; E1–E4 chosen on site): phone GPS (screenshot of
     coordinates and accuracy), 4 compass-oriented photos, close-up of ground/rock with scale bar, hand-
     lens photo, a 15 s D435 recording (`rs-record`) from ~1.5 m looking down ~60°, one line of notes:
     surface type (bare rock / thin soil / grass / forest litter), colour, texture (waxy/scaly/foliated),
     vegetation cover estimate, magnet response on a loose surface fragment *without removing it*.
  3. Processing: D435 point cloud → same plane-fit estimator at 0.5, 1 and 2 m → slope and roughness;
     compare with the lidar values in the CSV (expect agreement only on bare ground; vegetation raises
     D435 roughness).
- **Observation vs identification:** colour, sheen, scaliness and magnet response are *observations
  consistent with* serpentinite; they are not mineral identification and cannot confirm serpentinization.
- **Evidence that could challenge assumptions:** no exposed rock along the transect; no difference across
  the mapped contact; schist on the "ultramafic" side; D435 vs lidar disagreement on bare ground.
- **Backup (if no rock or D435 fails):** photo-and-GPS survey of surface type along the same stations,
  lidar profiles prepared in advance, and an honest statement of what could not be measured.
- **Safety and rules:** no hammering, collecting or entering old pits (amphibole asbestos occurs in these
  rocks; park rules), stay on trails, skip stations outside the park, wear blaze orange (hunting season),
  park opens 8 AM.

### P1-8 Nottingham–Jezero: what's supported vs hypothetical
- **Supported:** (a) Perseverance found in situ evidence of serpentinization and associated H₂ production
  in igneous rocks of the Máaz formation on the Jezero crater floor (Tosca et al. 2025, *Science Advances*,
  doi:10.1126/sciadv.adr8793), with Fe-rich serpentine as predicted, and pockets suggesting localized
  reaction; olivine–carbonate rocks occur in the Jezero region (Brown et al. 2020) and Séítah carbonates
  are magnesite–siderite solid solutions (Clavé et al. 2023). (b) Nottingham's bedrock is serpentinite
  with relict peridotite in the State Line complex, part of the Baltimore mafic complex (USGS Geolex), and
  the district produced magnesite and chromite.
- **Hypothetical / not equivalent:** Mars serpentine is Fe-rich and formed in an ancient basaltic crust;
  Nottingham is Mg-rich ultramafic rock serpentinized during Paleozoic mountain building, now weathered
  under a humid, vegetated climate. Same process family, different parent rocks, chemistry, fluids,
  timing and setting. Nottingham is not a lake, not a delta, not a landing site.
- **What terrestrial study can legitimately help with:** recognising serpentinite and its weathering
  products in the field and in images; testing instruments and workflows (e.g. depth cameras, spectra)
  on serpentinized rock; studying how serpentinite surfaces and soils look and erode. Frame the visit
  as "a targeted look at a regional serpentinite analog for a process Perseverance has documented".
